    How this board works

    Goal. Build the roster with the highest dependable weekly score, not the highest ceiling. Rankings favor players who post their median every week and stay on the field.

    This league. Nalas Football League, 12 teams, snake, 15 rounds. Starters: 1 QB, 2 RB, 2 WR, 1 TE, 2 FLEX, 1 K, 1 D/ST, plus 5 bench and 2 IR. Two flex slots mean roughly 5 of your 10 starters are RB/WR — the board already prices that in.

    Rel pts. Projected 2026 season points under your exact league scoring, tilted toward each player's proven weekly median (2025 weighted over 2024) and discounted for games missed. This is the number the board ranks by.

    CS (consistency). 0–100 within position: weekly median, floor (25th percentile week), week-to-week volatility, and games played across 2024–25. 70+ is steady, under 45 is thin evidence or boom/bust.

    Career. Points per game under this league's scoring across every NFL season played (data back to 2005), with total games. Context only — long-career rates mix eras and roles, so this column never moves the rankings.

    Value. Rel pts above the replacement starter at that position for your lineup settings. Superflex changes QB replacement automatically.

    Recommendations. Value × roster need × availability. A player unlikely to survive to your next pick gets a push; positions you have covered get discounted. K and D/ST are discounted for projection noise, but this league's kicker scoring (0.1/FG yard) makes elite kickers worth a round 13–14 pick.

    Badges. ST steady · VOL boom/bust · INJ missed time · R rookie · NT new team · EST projection estimated from history or market · VF verify starter status before drafting.

    Live sync. Run the ESPN helper script in your draft-room tab and picks arrive here automatically — it reads the pick panel as text, so names are exact. It only ever adds players, never removes, so a partial panel can't wipe your log. The pill in the top bar shows it's alive; if it says idle, click Refresh in the helper.

    Picking. Type a name, Enter drafts the top result to whichever team is on the clock (snake order tracks whose turn it is — including yours). ↑↓ choose a different row. / focuses search. Undo steps back one pick.

    Bye planning. The grid counts your starters off each week. Two same-position starters sharing a bye flags amber; three or more flags red.

    Draft flow. Pace is how fast each position is actually going in this room versus its ADP — 1.0 is on schedule, 1.4x means a run. It compares picks against the ADP density of the recent window, so back-loaded positions like K and D/ST don't read as runs just because they arrive late. A hot position stretches the availability window in scoring, which is what turns a run into a reason to take yours now.

    Room needs. How many rival teams still have an open slot a position can fill. On its own that number is uninformative early — everyone needs everything — so the board only flags a squeeze when startable supply has actually fallen below demand.

    PO (playoff slate). Weeks 15–17 schedule strength: 2025 fantasy points allowed by each opponent at that position, indexed so 1.00 is average and above 1.00 is generous. Last year's defence predicts this year's only weakly, so this is a tiebreaker between similar players and is weighted to move a score by only a few percent.

    K and D/ST plan. The two positions get opposite advice because the evidence differs. Kicker has a genuine unanimous #1 (Brandon Aubrey, K1 at all nine ranking sources checked) whose long-range profile this league's 0.1/yd FG scoring rewards more than standard scoring does — worth taking near ADP, not worth reaching for. Defence has no consensus #1 at all: three different teams hold the top spot across sources and under a point per game separates DST1 from DST10, so the panel points you at the weeks 1–4 schedule instead of the season projection.

    Depth chart. The Pos column shows each player's rank on his team's published depth chart — RB1, RB2, WR3 — pulled 30 Aug from ESPN's chart and cross-checked against Ourlads. An RB2 behind a healthy starter is a lottery ticket; an RB1 is a job, and the board now says which is which instead of inferring order from its own projections. Where two players share a rank the team itself has not picked a starter (Minnesota and New Orleans at RB, the Giants' RB2, the Chargers at TE), and the card says so. A ? means the player is not on the published chart and the rank was inferred. Handcuff detection follows the chart too, so it offers you the actual next man up.

    Which team is yours. ESPN marks your own column in its draft board, so the helper reads your slot straight from the page and the board sets itself — no more attributing someone else's roster to you. A bar under the top bar confirms the slot, and turns red if ESPN ever disagrees with the board. You can still set the slot by hand in Settings; doing so turns auto-detect off so a later sync can't undo your choice.

    ADP. The board plans against a blend of ESPN's own league ADP (70%) and FantasyFootballCalculator's (30%), because you are drafting on ESPN against people reading ESPN's rankings. The two populations diverge hard at the back of the board — ESPN drafters take Brandon Aubrey around pick 87 where the wider market has him at 138 — and a ▲ next to an ADP marks a player your room will take at least 25 picks earlier than the market would. Hover any ADP to see both numbers.

    Data. 2026 consensus projections (FantasyPros, CBS) rescored to your league settings; ADP from FantasyFootballCalculator half-PPR 12-team; 2023–25 weekly stats via nflverse; 2026 byes from the released schedule. Mid-round RBs and three mid-camp veteran WR signings carry per-player FantasyPros pulls from 29 Aug, so no player inside the top 150 is running on a history-derived guess. Injury and role notes were researched the same day.
